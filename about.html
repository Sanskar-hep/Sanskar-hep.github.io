About

Beyond the CV

I've always been fascinated by questions that don't have simple answers. What is the universe made of? Why do the fundamental particles behave the way they do? How can we extract tiny physical signals hidden within enormous amounts of experimental data? These are the questions that drew me to particle physics and continue to inspire the work I do today.

I recently graduated with a BS–MS degree in Physical Sciences from IISER Berhampur. My journey into experimental high-energy physics led me to the CMS Collaboration through my master's thesis at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR), Mumbai, where I worked on measuring collider-independent asymmetries in top-quark pair production using proton–proton collision data from the Large Hadron Collider.

For me, research is much more than writing code or analysing data. I enjoy understanding systems from first principles, breaking difficult problems into manageable pieces, and finding elegant solutions through careful reasoning. Whether I'm studying detector responses, implementing statistical methods, or exploring machine learning for collider analyses, I find the process of learning just as rewarding as the final result.

Outside of research, I enjoy building things that make science more accessible and organised—from analysis software and computational tools to this website, where I document my work and share what I'm learning along the way. I believe that good science should be reproducible, transparent, and clearly communicated.

As I continue toward a PhD in experimental particle physics, I hope to contribute to precision measurements, searches for physics beyond the Standard Model, and the development of new computational techniques that help us understand the universe at its most fundamental level. At the heart of it all, I'm simply someone who enjoys asking difficult questions—and is willing to spend a long time searching for meaningful answers.
